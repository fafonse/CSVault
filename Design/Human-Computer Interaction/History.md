## [Vannevar Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vannevar_Bush)
- The National Science Foundation (NSF) was founded in 1950 by him, and was a large contributor to the technological advancements of the 60's-80's.

In 1945 he publishes a short article proposing an information system called *Memex*. A portmanteau of *memory* and *index*, it was a mechanical system that would contain records that an individual could access with "exceeding speed and flexibility". Essentially it was [[Hyper Text Transfer Protocol|hypertext]] with index cards.

## [Grace Hopper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Hopper)

- Created *FORTRAN* and *COBOL*.
- Created the first compiler in early 1950s
	- A-0: Converts language to machine code
	- Made computers available to scientists and engineers, not just the computer experts

## Ivan Sutherland
- Pioneer in computer graphics
	- Was in University of Utah
- Created an early predecessor to the graphical user interface with *Sketchpad*

## DEC PDP-1
Created in 1961, a *smaller* big computer that could fit in a regular room. Could use a standard teletypewriter input/output along with a circle CRT (used to be used in radar).

## Doug Engelbart (THE GOAT)
- Founded Augmentation Research Center (at SRI)

- Researched and built computer-based tech for:
	- Manipulation information
	- Improving *individual* and *group processes* for ==knowledge work==
- Built the idea of the *computer mouse*
	- Needed something to move the *cursor* around the screen.
		- Out performed light pens, trackballs, joysticks in speed and accuracy
		- Keeps the cursor in place when you let go of the mouse
			- Great for usage with a keyboard
	- Was literally a block of wood

## Dynabook
- Created in 1971
- Proof of concept for a portable computer that could be used to connect to video networks or ARPA.
- Basically an iPad with a huge fucking keyboard stuck on.

## Xerox Alto
- First computer to really put Engelbert's ideas to use
- Not a huge success, but really amazing for the time
- Very expensive
## Apple 1
- Created in 1976
- Very much inspired by recent Xerox machines
- Created as a kit computer for hobbyists to use
	- Very cheap compared to enterprise solutions
- **Beginning of personal computing**
## Xerox Star
- Created in 1981
- Big brother to the *Alto*
- A response to the Apple revolution
## Macintosh
- Created in 1984
- A response to the *Xerox Star*
- Really the first personal computer to come with a GUI
- Absolutely fucked Xerox
- Cheap compared to literally everyone else
