Typically, you don't have time for the [[Ethnography|classic]] way of learning about the users. Instead, we take a more active role in learning about how users work with designs.

- Ask *how*, and *what* to get the *why*
- Shotgun questions about how people use their products
	- People summarize, but we need details
- You get a lot of facts, but you need the *correct interpretations*!
	- Share your own interpretations to check your reasoning
	- EX: "So by keeping people accountable, you mean keeping a paper trail?"
- Keep it concrete when people start abstracting
	- Watch for "generally", "we usually"...
	- "We usually get reports by email", ask "Can I see one?"
- Anything you don't understand fully, ask!
	- "How do you know this is high priority"
	- "Who marks it as high priority"
	- "Why is it high priority?"
	- "What do you do compared to a low priority item?"

## Paradigm Shifts
Sometimes, you need to learn to shift your design towards the customer. People don't do things for no reason, and you must understand what the root cause of their behavior is to make a good design decision. 

> Don't put window systems in mobile phone designs, why use 2D UI elements in VR?
