## History

Modern fragmentation can be further broken down into [[Unit Operations|unit operations]] of:
- **Drilling:** Cutting or punching a narrow hole into the rock
- **Blasting:** Using explosives or other methods to create a *dynamic load* on the rock causing it to break.
### History of Drilling
#### Hand Jacking
Happened up to the late 1800s. Just punching into rock by hand, no mechanical power.

#### Pneumatic Drilling
By the 1890s, the first pneumatic drill came out. It was useful for its ability to be anchored and it's increased power.

#### Rotary Drilling
Idea is to rotate your *chisel* instead of using it percussively. The first rotary rig was developed in the early 1880s to drill in shallow water wells.
> Commonly used on oilfields

#### Black Powder
Black powder was a very dangerous explosive. It requires an open flame to ignite, is unpredictable, unreliable, and produces a shit ton of smoke. Also it'll go off on any loose flame.

**Fire Boss**: A person designated to examine the mine for gas and other dangers. In some states, the fire boss was the designated mine examiner.
In the early days of mining the fire boss would wear thick clothing soaked in water, and they would just walk through the mine with a candle on a long stick. If there was pockets of gas the candle would ignite them, often times killing the fire boss.
#### Dynamite
Dynamite is still considered safe to this day. Nobel was the guy who invented it, and turned nitroglycerin into a relatively safe explosive. Dynamite and blasting caps together changed up explosives safety forever.

#### ANFO
**A**mmonium **N**itrate **F**uel **O**il

Consists of 94% porous drilled ammonium nitrate, which acts as the oxidizing and absorbent for the fuel, and 6% number 2 fuel oil.
## Symbols
#### St. Barbara
The old silver city of Kutna Hora in Bohemia originally showed a figure of St. Barbara above the crossed hammer and gad on its coat of arms.

## Hammer and Gad
Usually seen as together in a cross pattern. Classic tools of the trade, they can be seen all over German coat of arms.
> The gad is just a chisel with a handle. Think like a tiny, half pickaxe.
