You let the mine cave behind you as you go along. This makes it so that you don't need to support every tunnel mined, and it can help prevent huge cave ins from occuring.

You need the right rock to do it, but you rely on the rock *caving in* when you remove supports.

> [More info](https://www.geoengineer.org/news/block-caving-a-new-mining-method-arises)

**Advantages**:
- Inexpensive
	- Relatively little drilling, blasting, and timbering per ton
- Centralized production permits
	- Efficient supervision, safety control
- Ventilation relatively simple
- Production can be maintained at a consistently high rate
- Suitable to low grade orebodies

**Disadvantages**:
- Expensive start-up costs
- [[Drift]] maintenance can be costly
- Slow response to changing production requirements
	- Cannot change to another [[Mining Methods|mining method]] easily
- Low ore recovery in adverse conditions
- Large amounts of ore is lost when good practice is not followed


## Dangers
- Can create airgaps within the mine
- Can also cave in entire mountains
	- Large chunks can fall and create extreme pressure waves in the rock
- You end up with an open pit above the caving
