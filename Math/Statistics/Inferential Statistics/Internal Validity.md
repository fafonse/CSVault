Are your findings actually *valid*? Did you use the correct data in the correct way and announce the correct findings?

## Threats
There are several factors we have to consider when we check our data for validity. This can happen when we're comparing temporal samples of a single population as well as comparing groups/populations to each other.
- **History**
	- Have any historical events impacted your data, or made you reconsider your findings?
- **Maturation**
	- Did the testing population mature between measurements? Is the rate of maturation different between groups?
- **Testing**
	- Did gathering your data in some way change the original population?
	- *Don't measure the harm of smoking by telling a bunch of non-smokers to start*
- **Instrumentation**
	- Did the testing methods/measurement methods change between groups?
- **Regression**
	- Were we already in the middle of a trend? Was it a record high before, and the only way was down?
- **Mortality**
	- Did the populations change between sampling? (People leaving area, deaths, redistricting)

We also have to consider *social interactions*. People don't exist in isolation, they react other's behavior. 
- **Diffusion/Imitation of treatment**
	- Groups copy each other
- **Compensatory Rivalry**
	- Groups try to rival each other in their numbers
- **Resentful Demoralization**
	- Groups see each other's results and are demoralized
- **Compensatory Equalization**
	- Groups are unequal, and a third party decides to help equalize the results between them
