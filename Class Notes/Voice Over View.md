Beginning with out first Bake Off results, we decided on a couple of different approaches to input. 

*show paper prototypes*

We came up with the ideas of using the keyboard to interact with the target, 
applying arrows and highlights to mark the target after, 
audio queues to indicate a target, 
recentering the cursor after the target is clicked,
and using the release of a user's finger from the touchpad to interact with the target.

Our prototypes as a whole can be categorized as either guiding the users to target cubes before they're shown or increasing the speed of input.

As our prototype ideas were accepted, we moved on with a practical implementation. 

Here we combined our two categories of ideas into 3 prototypes: 
Prototype 1: Release to Click, cursor recentering, and highlighting the next cube.
Prototype 2: Arrows to targets and Keyboard interaction.
and Prototype 3: Highlight next cube and Keyboard click.

After evaluating our prototypes and testing them ourselves, we came to the conclusion that some combination of P2 and P3 were best. 
Testing had showed that the release to click action in P1 was inaccurate and often lead to *cursor overshoot*. The keyboard action however was much more intuitive, as selecting the target was a physically separate action from interacting with the target.

With our findings from the previous bakeoffs, we have now optimized our design to be more intuitive and efficient for users.

*start playing demonstration of the final design*

For our final prototype, we have decided to combine the highlight and arrow methods for guiding users to targets. They can coexist at the same time, and the highlight makes the next target cube more clear. 
The keyboard click will also remain, and we have decided on setting the interaction key bind to TAB or BACKSPACE, which are far away from the touchpad position and add more physical separation to the actions.
